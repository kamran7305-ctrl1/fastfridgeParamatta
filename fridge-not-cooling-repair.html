Quick Check

Is Your Fridge Really Not Cooling?

Before you book, check these signs. Any of them points to a cooling fault:

  • The fridge compartment is warm, but the freezer is still frozen
  • Both the fridge and freezer are warm
  • The inside temperature is above 5°C
  • The compressor runs constantly but doesn't cool
  • The fridge is silent and doesn't run at all
  • Food spoils faster than normal
  • Ice has built up at the back of the freezer
  • The back or sides of the fridge feel unusually hot

Use a fridge thermometer. Safe temperatures are 3–4°C in the fridge and −18°C in the freezer. If your fridge stays above 5°C, don't wait: bacteria grow quickly above that.

Fridge Condenser Repair in Parramatta

The condenser is the part that releases heat from your fridge. It sits at the back or underneath the fridge, with a fan to push air across it. When it can't get rid of heat, the whole system struggles and the fridge stops cooling properly.

Our fridge condenser repair in Parramatta covers:

  • Condenser coil cleaning: dust, fluff and pet hair insulate the coils and trap heat.
  • Condenser fan motor replacement: a dead fan means no airflow across the coils.
  • Blocked or restricted airflow: a fridge crammed into a tight cavity can overheat.
  • Leaking coils or pipework: refrigerant leaks need to be found, repaired and the system regassed by an ARC-licensed technician.
  • Compressor stress caused by overheating: we check the compressor for damage before it fails.

Signs You Need Fridge Condenser Repair

  • The back or base of the fridge is very hot
  • The compressor runs for hours without switching off
  • The fridge is noisy, with a buzzing or whirring fan
  • Power bills are climbing
  • The fridge struggles in hot weather

If you want to try basic maintenance first, our guide to cleaning fridge condenser coils on the Summer Fridge Maintenance page explains how. If cleaning doesn't help, a technician can test the fan and refrigerant circuit.

Fridge Evaporator Repair in Parramatta

The evaporator is the cold coil inside your freezer compartment. A fan pulls warm air across it, and it absorbs the heat. In a frost-free fridge-freezer, the freezer's evaporator fan also blows cold air into the fridge. When it fails, the fridge warms up while the freezer may still look fine.

Our fridge evaporator repair in Parramatta service includes:

  • Evaporator fan motor replacement: one of the most common reasons a fridge stops cooling.
  • Defrost heater, timer and sensor repairs: if they fail, frost builds up on the coil and blocks airflow.
  • Evaporator coil icing: we find why the ice formed, rather than just melting it.
  • Drain and air duct clearing: blocked drains can freeze and block airflow.
  • Evaporator coil leaks: diagnosed and quoted honestly, as some leaks may not be worth repairing on older fridges.

Signs You Need Fridge Evaporator Repair

  • The freezer is cold, but the fridge is warm
  • A thick layer of frost or ice on the freezer's back wall
  • A loud buzzing, rattling or clicking from inside the freezer
  • Water pooling at the bottom of the fridge
  • Cooling that comes and goes

Ice on the evaporator is a symptom, not the cause. Defrosting it only gives temporary relief, so the underlying defrost fault still needs repairing or the problem will return.

Other Faults We Repair

Other Fridge Not Cooling Faults We Repair

Thermostat and Temperature Sensor Faults

A faulty thermostat or sensor tells the fridge it's cold when it isn't, so the compressor switches off too soon. These are inexpensive parts and usually quick to replace.

Compressor and Start Relay Problems

A clicking sound, a hot compressor or no cooling at all can point to the start relay or overload protector. Replacing them is often straightforward. A failed compressor is more serious, and we'll tell you honestly whether repair makes sense for the age of your fridge. See our fridge compressor repair page.

Refrigerant Leaks and Regassing

If the system is low on refrigerant, the fridge runs but doesn't cool. Simply topping up isn't a lasting fix. We locate and repair the leak first, using ARC-licensed technicians, then regas the system.

Control Board Faults

Modern fridges rely on electronic control boards. A faulty board can stop the compressor, the fans or the defrost cycle. We test the board before replacing it, so you don't pay for parts you don't need.

Door Seals and Airflow Problems

Worn door gaskets let warm air in, and overloaded shelves block vents. A fridge that's mostly fine mechanically can still struggle to cool because of these. See our fridge door seal replacement page.

Before You Call

What To Check Before You Call

These quick checks may solve the problem, or at least help us diagnose faster:

  1. 1 Is it plugged in, and is the power point working? Test it with another appliance.
  2. 2 Has the breaker tripped? Reset it once only.
  3. 3 Check the temperature setting. Someone may have changed it.
  4. 4 Clear the vents. Don't block the air vents in the fridge and freezer.
  5. 5 Check the door seal. Close the door on a sheet of paper. If it slides out, the seal needs attention.
  6. 6 Look at the back of the fridge. If it's covered in dust, a clean might help.
  7. 7 Check for ventilation. Leave a gap around the sides, top and back.
  8. 8 Keep the doors shut. This protects your food while you wait.

If the problem continues, please don't dismantle the fridge. Electrical faults can be dangerous, and refrigerant work legally needs an ARC-licensed technician.

How Long Can Food Stay Safe?

As a general guide, a closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours without cooling. After that, food kept above 5°C may not be safe. Move meat, dairy and leftovers to an esky with ice if your fridge will be down longer. Frozen food stays frozen for much longer if the freezer door stays closed. When in doubt, throw it out. See the NSW Food Authority for official advice.

Is It Worth Repairing a Fridge That Isn't Cooling?

Often, yes. Fans, thermostats, sensors and relays are inexpensive compared with a new fridge. As a general rule:

  • Repair if your fridge is under about 8 years old and the repair costs less than half the price of a comparable new one.
  • Think carefully if the fridge is over 10 years old and needs a major repair, like a compressor or sealed system work.
  • Replace if it's very old, rusty, or has failed repeatedly.

Our technicians will give you honest advice, even if that means saying your fridge isn't worth fixing. For more help, read our guide, Repair or Replace Your Fridge?
