Common Fridge and Freezer Problems We Fix

Recognise the symptom and you'll know what to tell us when you book.

Fridge not cooling but the light is on

Often a dirty condenser coil, a failed evaporator fan, a faulty start relay or low refrigerant. The compartment feels warm even though the unit sounds like it's running normally.

Fridge not cooling but freezer works

A classic airflow or damper fault the freezer is fine, but cold air isn't reaching the fridge section. Extremely common on frost-free models, and usually caused by a blocked evaporator, an iced-over coil or a dead circulation fan.

Freezer not freezing

Usually a defrost system fault, blocked airflow or a struggling compressor. Ice cream softens and frozen goods start to sweat before anything else gives it away.

Ice build-up in the freezer

A defrost heater, defrost timer or sensor fault or a worn door gasket letting humid air in that freezes onto the coils and slowly chokes airflow.

Fridge leaking water inside or on the floor

Typically a blocked defrost drain or a faulty ice-maker line. Water pools under the crisper or spreads across the floor. One of the cheapest fixes on this list when it's caught early.

Fridge making loud noise, buzzing or clicking

Rattling or buzzing usually points to a failing condenser or evaporator fan motor. Repeated loud clicking every few minutes often means a struggling start relay or a compressor trying and failing to start.

Compressor running constantly

Dirty coils, a leaking door seal or low refrigerant force the compressor to run non-stop. You'll notice extra heat around the unit and a noticeably higher power bill.

Fridge too cold or freezing food

A failed thermostat, thermistor or temperature sensor leaves the fridge freezing lettuce and milk unpredictably while still reporting the correct setting.

Fridge tripping the power or circuit breaker

Usually a compressor winding fault, a failed capacitor, or moisture in the defrost heater circuit. Stop using the appliance and book a technician this one is an electrical safety issue.

Error codes on the display

Modern fridges flash fault codes for sensor, fan, defrost, inverter and communication errors. We read and interpret them against manufacturer service data.

Door seal and gasket problems

Perished or torn gaskets let cold escape, driving up running costs and causing sweating, condensation and ice. A cheap repair with an outsized payoff.

Display panel or control board issues

Unresponsive or glitching control panels, touch displays and inverter boards often a connection or board fault we can test and repair.

Fridge smells bad

Usually a blocked drain holding stagnant water, or mould in the drip tray. Cleanable, but persistent odours can also point to an overheating component.

Should You Repair or Replace Your Fridge?

We'd rather give you the truth than sell you a repair you'll regret in six months. Here's the rule of thumb we apply when we diagnose your fridge.

Situation Usually Worth Repairing?
Fridge under ~8 years old with a common part fault (fan, thermostat, seal, relay) Yes a straightforward, cost-effective fix
Mid-range fridge, repair cost under roughly half the replacement price Usually especially with a warranty on the work
Premium French door, integrated or built-in unit Often replacement is expensive, so repair frequently makes sense
Older budget fridge (10+ years) with a failed compressor or sealed-system leak Often not the fix approaches the cost of a new unit
Ageing unit with repeated, recurring faults Consider replacing we'll give you a candid view
Any unit still under manufacturer warranty Contact the manufacturer first don't void your cover

The average lifespan of a refrigerator in Australia is roughly 10 to 15 years, though a well-maintained unit in a shaded, ventilated spot can comfortably exceed that. Age, the specific fault, energy efficiency and repair cost against a new unit all matter. When we assess your fridge we'll tell you plainly which way we'd go if it were ours and we only recommend replacement when it's genuinely the better value.

Preventative Maintenance Tips for Parramatta's Climate

A little upkeep goes a long way in Western Sydney heat especially heading into summer.

Residential Fridges & Freezers

Vacuum the condenser coils twice a year, keep the unit at least 5cm off the wall for airflow, check door seals for cracks by closing them on a sheet of paper, and don't overpack air needs room to circulate to cool evenly.

Commercial Refrigeration

Schedule regular coil cleaning and gasket checks, keep condensers clear of kitchen grease, and log temperatures daily so a slow drift is caught long before it becomes a breakdown.

Coolrooms

Inspect door seals and closers, keep evaporators clear of ice, check drain lines, and book periodic servicing a coolroom failure is far cheaper to prevent than to recover from.

Display Fridges

Clean glass, fans and drains regularly, and never block internal vents with stock. Restricted airflow is the single most common reason display units lose temperature.

Freezers

Defrost before ice gets thicker than about 6mm, check the seal, and keep the unit out of direct heat and afternoon sun wherever you can.
