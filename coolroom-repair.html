What We Repair

What We Fix in Coolrooms and Cold Rooms

A coolroom is a system: room, door, evaporator, condensing unit and controls. A fault in any one part can warm the whole room.

Doors, hinges, seals and strip curtains

Worn seals and sagging doors let warm air in, so the unit runs flat out. A common and cheap fix that saves a lot of power.

Evaporator coils and fans

Ice, failed fan motors and blocked drains inside the room stop cold air moving.

Condensing units and compressors

The outdoor or rooftop unit that does the heavy lifting: fans, contactors, pressure controls and compressors.

Controllers, probes and alarms

Temperature controllers, sensors and alarm panels that read wrong or trip for no clear reason.

Defrost systems and heaters

Defrost heaters, timers and drain-line heaters that stop ice building up, especially in freezer rooms.

Gas leaks and regas

Leaks are found and fixed first, then the system is recharged by weight with the right refrigerant.

When It Fails

Coolroom Breakdown? What to Do Before We Arrive

Cold food should be held at 5°C or below. These steps buy you time.

Do this

  • Keep the door closed. Do not prop it open to "air" the room.
  • Read the room temperature on a probe thermometer, not just the controller.
  • Move meat, dairy, seafood and prepared food to a working fridge or a cooler with ice.
  • Check that the condensing unit isolator or breaker has not tripped.
  • Note any alarm or controller code, and the brand on the condensing unit plate.

Avoid this

  • Do not chip ice off the evaporator. A punctured coil is a costly repair.
  • Do not add refrigerant yourself. It needs an ARC licence.
  • Do not keep resetting the controller. It can hide the fault.
  • If you smell burning or see sparks at the condensing unit, switch it off at the isolator and call us.

Food above 5°C for more than 4 hours should be thrown out. Need us out of hours? See emergency fridge repair.

Coolroom Maintenance

Cool Room Maintenance That Prevents Breakdowns

Most coolroom failures build up slowly. A regular visit finds them early.

What a visit covers

  • Condensing unit coil and fan cleaning
  • Door seals, hinges, closer and strip curtain
  • Evaporator coil, fan and drain line
  • Defrost cycle and heaters
  • Controller, probe and temperature alarm test

Easy checks between visits

  • Keep stock off the floor and away from the evaporator.
  • Keep a clear gap around the condensing unit so air can move.
  • Look for ice around the door and on the coil.
  • Test that the door closes by itself and the seal grips.
