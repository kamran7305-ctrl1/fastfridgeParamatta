When It Fails

Commercial Fridge Not Cooling? What to Do in the First Hour

Food safety rules say cold food should be held at 5°C or below. These steps protect your stock while you wait for us.

Do this now

  • Keep the doors shut. Every opening lets cold out.
  • Check the temperature with a probe thermometer, not the display.
  • Move meat, dairy, seafood and prepared food into a working fridge or a cooler with ice first.
  • Look for a tripped breaker, a unit that has been unplugged or a door left open.
  • Write down any error code and the brand and model, then tell us when you book.

Do not do this

  • Do not keep resetting the unit. It can hide the fault from the technician.
  • Do not chip ice off the evaporator with a tool. A punctured coil is an expensive repair.
  • Do not top up gas yourself. Handling refrigerant needs an ARC licence.
  • If you smell burning or hear sparking, switch it off at the wall and call us.

Food that has sat above 5°C for more than 4 hours should be thrown out. If it is after hours, go straight to emergency fridge repair.

Prevent Breakdowns

Commercial Refrigeration Maintenance

Most commercial fridge breakdowns are avoidable. A short, regular visit costs far less than a failed unit and a bin full of stock.

What a maintenance visit covers

  • Condenser coil cleaning, the biggest cause of overheating
  • Door seal and hinge checks
  • Fan motor and defrost system checks
  • Temperature and thermostat checks against what the display says
  • Drain and drip tray checks

Easy wins you can do yourself

  • Leave a gap around the unit so air can move.
  • Do not stack stock against the evaporator or air vents.
  • Wipe door seals and make sure doors close fully.
  • Vacuum dust from accessible condenser grilles with the power off.

Want regular visits for your venue? Tell us what you run when you book and we will quote it. For basic coil cleaning, our fridge maintenance tips show how.
